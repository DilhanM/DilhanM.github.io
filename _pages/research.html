Our research sits at the boundary of quantum information science and chemical physics. We are interested both in developing new quantum algorithms suited to near-term hardware, and in using them — alongside classical tensor-network and machine-learning methods — to tackle problems in molecular simulation that are hard for classical computers alone.

Quantum algorithms for chemistry

Designing and compiling quantum circuits for simulating chemical processes — including reaction kinetics, proton-transfer dynamics, and molecular structure optimisation — with an eye towards what is actually implementable on current and near-term quantum processors.

Quantum machine learning

Encoding molecular structure into quantum machine learning models, and exploring where quantum-enhanced representations offer a genuine advantage over classical approaches for chemistry and materials prediction tasks.

Tensor-network methods

Matrix product states, DMRG, and related tensor-network techniques for simulating excited-state and photoexcited dynamics in molecular systems — particularly relevant to photochemistry and light-harvesting materials such as carotenoids.

Applications

  • Photoexcited-state dynamics and singlet fission in organic chromophores
  • Quantum algorithms for protein structure and side-chain optimisation
  • Molecular recognition and host–guest chemistry (ion-pair recognition, metal–organic frameworks)
